## 1. The anchor: did we reproduce OGBench?

Stock OGBench MLP (3×512, GELU, `latent_dim=512`), published flags, 3 seeds, 1M steps.
Metric is the mean of the last three evaluations, which is what the OGBench paper reports.

| agent | ours (last-3 @ 1M, 15 eps) | published (50 eps) | seeds |
|---|---|---|---|
| CRL | **45.5 ± 8.1** | 53 ± 6 | 56.9, 40.9, 38.7 |
| GCBC | **37.2 ± 11.4** | 45 ± 11 | 23.6, 51.6, 36.4 |

Both land ~8 points low, both with a seed spread wide enough that the intervals overlap
the published ones. With 3 seeds and a SD near 10 points the standard error is ~5–6
points, so **this neither confirms nor refutes the reproduction**. It is consistent with
it. The honest read is that 3 seeds at 15 episodes cannot resolve an 8-point gap, and
the pending 50-episode re-eval is the thing that will move this either way.

Two mechanical caveats on the anchor, both verified in the configs:

- `plateau_min_steps` (1M) equals `step_cap` (1M), so plateau stopping could not fire
  early. All six runs took the full 1M steps. Four of them nonetheless report
  `stop_reason: "plateau"` — a cosmetic mislabel, since the condition is evaluated at
  the same step the cap ends at. The step counts are correct; only the label is wrong.
- `code_commit` on the anchor runs reads `0009db4`, but their configs contain the
  published flags added in `6e01598`. Both are true: the flags were in the working tree
  before that tree was committed. **We do not record whether the tree was dirty**, so
  `code_commit` alone does not identify the code that ran. Worth fixing before any
  result is written up.

## 2. The headline: depth helps CRL, and keeps helping

Residual tower, width 256, `repr_dim` 64, published flags, 3 seeds. Measured at a fixed
1M gradient steps for every run — necessary because the plateau rule stops runs anywhere
between 0.9M and 1.9M steps, and an unpinned last-3 would compare different points on
different curves.

| depth | CRL | GCBC |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 34.1 ± 9.8 | 53.5 ± 6.8 |
| 8 | 49.3 ± 3.8 | 65.2 ± 1.6 |
| 16 | **74.2 ± 5.1** \* | 62.5 ± 2.4 |
| 32 | not yet at 1M \* | 61.2 ± 5.5 |

\* CRL d16 has passed 1M steps so its value is final at that step, but the runs are
still training. CRL d32 is at ~280k steps and is excluded entirely rather than reported
at the wrong step.

Two separate findings:

**CRL scales with depth and does not saturate by 16.** 34 → 49 → 74 is a 40-point swing
across a 4× depth change, far outside the seed spread. At depth 16 CRL reaches 74%,
versus OGBench's published 53% for the same agent on the same dataset.

**GCBC saturates at depth 8.** 53.5 → 65.2 → 62.5 → 61.2. The gain from 4 to 8 is real
(SDs of 6.8 and 1.6, non-overlapping); everything past 8 is flat. This is the shape the
1000-layer paper's own GCBC rebuttal numbers suggest (47% at depth 2 → 63% at depth 32),
and our depth-32 GCBC of 61.2 sits essentially on their 63.

So the depth benefit here is **specific to the contrastive critic**, not to "deeper
networks on this dataset". A deeper BC policy plateaus; a deeper value function does not.

## 3. What the aggregate hides: depth unlocks one specific task

`antmaze-medium-stitch` evaluates on 5 goal-reaching tasks. Averaging them conceals that
the methods solve **different** tasks, and that depth acts on one task in particular.

Mean of last 3 evaluations, averaged over seeds:

| config | task 1 | task 2 | task 3 | task 4 | task 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CRL d4 (residual) | 0.10 | 0.43 | 0.83 | 0.13 | 0.16 |
| CRL d8 | 0.04 | 0.16 | 0.87 | 0.49 | 0.94 |
| CRL d16 \* | 0.39 | 0.64 | 0.95 | **0.80** | 0.93 |
| GCBC d4 | 0.94 | 0.60 | 0.24 | **0.00** | 0.54 |
| GCBC d8 | 0.95 | 0.70 | 0.52 | **0.00** | 0.93 |
| GCBC d16 | 0.94 | 0.82 | 0.39 | **0.00** | 0.96 |
| GCBC d32 \* | 0.90 | 0.82 | 0.39 | 0.10 | 0.89 |
| CRL d4 (stock MLP, anchor) | 0.74 | 0.69 | 0.10 | **0.01** | 0.74 |
| GCBC d4 (stock MLP, anchor) | 0.78 | 0.33 | 0.19 | **0.00** | 0.56 |

Three things fall out:

1. **Task 4 is solved only by a deep contrastive critic.** It is 0.00 for GCBC at every
   depth and every seed, and ~0.00 for both agents under the stock architecture. CRL
   with the residual tower climbs 0.13 → 0.49 → 0.80 as depth goes 4 → 8 → 16. This is a
   phase transition on one task, not a uniform lift, and it is most of CRL's aggregate
   gain. Because a single unsolved task caps the aggregate at 0.80, the published ~0.53
   for CRL is *consistent with task 4 being unsolved in the original runs too*.

2. **Architecture reallocates which tasks get solved, at nearly equal aggregate.** Stock
   CRL d4 scores 0.74 on task 1 and 0.10 on task 3; residual CRL d4 does the reverse —
   0.10 and 0.83. Aggregates: 45.5 vs 34.1. The aggregate difference is small; the
   per-task difference is near-total inversion. **Any conclusion drawn from the
   aggregate alone would have missed this.**

3. **CRL and GCBC are not ranked, they are complementary.** GCBC owns task 1 (0.90–0.95
   everywhere); CRL barely touches it until depth 16. CRL owns tasks 3 and 4. That makes
   a single "which method is better" number on this dataset close to meaningless.

Caveat on point 2: the anchor and the depth-4 residual run differ in **three** ways at
once — tower (plain vs residual), width (512 vs 256), and `repr_dim` (512 vs 64). The
inversion is real but unattributed. This is open question **Q2** biting, and it needs a
one-factor-at-a-time run to resolve.

## 4. The critic is learning better and generalizing worse

CRL critic diagnostics, all depths pinned to 1M steps (depth 32 excluded, not yet there):

| depth | InfoNCE top-1 acc | train contrastive loss | **val** contrastive loss | success |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 0.369 | 0.00387 | 0.00530 | 0.341 |
| 8 | 0.467 | 0.00337 | 0.00549 | 0.493 |
| 16 | 0.583 | 0.00269 | 0.00632 | 0.742 |

Train loss falls monotonically, validation loss **rises** monotonically, and downstream
success rises with it. The generalization gap on the contrastive objective widens by 50%
from depth 4 to 16 while success more than doubles.

The practical consequence: **held-out contrastive loss is anti-correlated with policy
performance here.** Using it for model selection, early stopping, or depth selection
would pick exactly the wrong network. Our plateau rule keys off evaluation success, not
validation loss, so it is unaffected — but this is worth stating explicitly before anyone
reaches for the obvious-looking validation metric.

(`binary_accuracy` is reported but near-useless: with batch 1024 the pair matrix is
~1/1024 positives, so predicting "negative" everywhere already scores 0.999. All depths
sit between 0.9991 and 0.9993. The figure labels it as such.)

Depth and parameter count are confounded in this grid — width is fixed at 256, so
deeper also means bigger. A width-matched parameter control is **not** in the grid. The
`plain_mlp` arch preset isolates residual-vs-plain but not depth-vs-size.

Note GCBC d16 (1.08M params, 62.5% success) beats anchor CRL d4 (3.79M params, 45.5%) at
a third the parameters and a quarter the wall clock.

## 6. What this changes

- **Q1 (critic loss form) is now more urgent, not less.** The depth effect is in the
  critic, which is exactly the component Q1 is about. `q1_loss_pilot` is configured and
  unrun.
- **Q2 (representation dim) is now blocking interpretation**, not just configuration.
  Section 3 point 2 cannot be attributed without it.
- The depth sweep should extend past 16 for CRL. d16 has not saturated and d32 is only
  14% through.
- Phase 2 probes should bin per-task, not just per-distance. Task 4 is the signal.

## 7. Known defects in the reporting path, found while compiling this

All four were found by checking output rather than trusting it, and all four are fixed in
`plot_results.py` as of this writing:

1. `fig3` looked for `train/categorical_accuracy`; the column is
   `train/critic/categorical_accuracy`. Every metric looked absent and the function
   returned without drawing **or warning**. The critic diagnostics in §4 did not exist
   until this was fixed. It now prints a warning naming the columns it did find.
2. `fig3` took each run's *last* logged value, which compared a depth-32 run at 280k
   steps against a depth-4 run at 1.9M and labelled both "final". Now pinned to a common
   step, with under-trained depths omitted and named in the caption.
3. `fig1` plotted in-flight runs with the same markers as finished ones, so a depth-32
   point at 14% progress read as a result. In-flight depths are now hollow markers on a
   dotted line and are excluded from the solid series.
4. `fig4` drew ticks from the full `GRID_DEPTHS` constant, leaving an empty slot at 64,
   and repeated the same legend in all three panels.

Defect 1 is the one that mattered: a figure that silently produces nothing is worse than
one that crashes, because the absence looks like a decision.
